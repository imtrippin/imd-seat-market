# SeatVault: an NFT-holding vault that enforces reward sharing (design, 2026-09-28, vault-only since 2026-09-29)

Local prototype only. It has been rehearsed against mocks on Base Sepolia (`contracts/script/testnet-walkthrough.sh`, throwaway keys) and reviewed three times; nothing has been paired with IMD, deployed on mainnet or funded with real value.

## What the developer's information enables, and what stays unverified

The IMD developer says pairing accepts a seat held by a contract that implements ERC-1271 (`isValidSignature(bytes32, bytes)` returning `0x1626ba7e`), and that rewards go to the holder, whatever it is. If both hold, an NFT held by a vault receives that seat's rewards directly, and the vault can split every reward-token transfer that reaches it by immutable terms. That is the one thing this project could not enforce before: **enforceable allocation of rewards that actually arrive**.

Still unverified, because no end-to-end contract-holder pairing has been run: that `/pair/complete` really calls `isValidSignature` with the plain EIP-712 digest (`IdentityMD Worker` v2, chain id, NFT contract) and passes the signature bytes through unchanged; the exact shape of `GET /pair/:code` (the script reads `deviceKey`, `nonce`, `relayOrigin`, `chainId`, `nftContract`, which the public docs snapshot does not list); how IMD's off-chain bind of seat to agent (`POST /agents/bind`) behaves for a vault-held seat (the on-chain registration itself is settled from public data and a fork run, see the registration section below); that the reward distributor keeps paying the current holder when the holder is a contract, and at which moment the holder is sampled; and how promptly a device goes stale after the NFT leaves the vault. The smallest test that settles most of this is one spare seat NFT in one vault on mainnet, with a throwaway operator key: pair, observe `/seats/:tokenId/standing`, register the agent through the vault, wait for one payout, settle and claim, then withdraw the NFT and watch the device go stale. Each step is a separately approved live action.

## Chosen design: the vault alone

One contract with one job: **SeatVault** holds exactly one seat NFT for one agreement, answers ERC-1271 for owner-approved pairings, splits arriving reward tokens by immutable basis points, and gives the NFT back to the owner on demand. A factory pins the collection, reward token, IMD's registrar and relay so a vault cannot be misconfigured by the parties.

There is no fee and no deposit. A rental escrow (a daily fee accrued per second and drawn from an owner deposit) was built and reviewed beside the vault and then removed on 2026-09-29 for simplicity; it lives in git history and could return as an optional add-on for listings that want a fee. Without it the host's only enforced protection is the split of what reaches the vault: an idle seat pays nothing, and the owner may withdraw at any moment, including right before a payout. Hosts price those risks into their percentage and their reputation; a minimum term or a cancellation charge would be a new economic rule with its own review.

## Custody

The vault is the on-chain holder: the owner `deposit()`s (a real `safeTransferFrom` into the vault; an approval alone does nothing) and only the agreed seat is accepted from the seat collection, only from the owner, only once; any other collection's token is accepted and rescuable (`rescueERC721`), and any ERC-20 other than the reward token is the owner's to take back (`rescueERC20`); neither rescue can name the seat, the reward token or the registrar. The owner keeps the withdrawal right: `withdrawNFT(to)` whenever the vault actually holds the seat, before or after the agreement ended, whether it arrived by deposit or by a plain transfer, with no provider signature and no admin. Withdrawal makes no call to the reward token: it ends the agreement, clears the pairing approval and moves the NFT, so nothing about the reward asset (a revert, malformed return data, a gas-burning read) can delay the seat's return. Rewards already in the vault stay allocated to the same immutable split, and anyone can settle them before or after. The provider can `end()` (no new pairings) once the owner has recorded the seat as held (by `deposit()` or `syncHeld()`), never before on either deposit path, and can never move the NFT or any token but its own allocation of the reward token. Withdrawals and rescues refuse the zero address. There is no admin key and no upgrade; the only sweeps are the owner's rescues.

## Pairing authority (ERC-1271), exactly

- The vault keeps a single active approval. `approvePairing(nonce, expiresAt, relayOrigin)` by the owner computes the WorkerAuthorization digest itself from the agreed device key, its own address as `wallet`, its token, IMD's nonce, the expiry and the agreed relay, and records it with its expiry and the chain id, replacing any earlier approval. It refuses another relay, an expiry in the past or more than one hour out, a vault that does not hold the NFT, or an ended agreement. Changing the device key, revoking, ending or withdrawing clears the approval.
- `isValidSignature(hash, signature)` returns the magic value only when: the NFT is held, the agreement is open, `hash` is the active approved digest, not expired, approved on this chain id, and the signature recovers to the operator key (the host's pairing key) or the owner. The operator key must differ from both the owner's and the provider's keys, or the vault refuses to be created. Anything else returns `0xffffffff` without reverting. Consequences: no arbitrary hash is ever valid, so the operator key cannot be used to sign a token approval or a transfer; a digest approved for one nonce cannot be replayed for another; a digest from another chain, relay, device, wallet or token is a different hash and is never approved; after exit or withdrawal nothing validates; a leaked operator key can at most complete a pairing the owner already approved.
- Revoking an approval, ending, or changing the device key only prevents new pairings. A device IMD already enrolled stays enrolled until IMD sees the holder change, which is what withdrawing the NFT does. Nothing in the vault disconnects a device.
- Two clocks. The vault only knows the signature expiry it approved (at most one hour); IMD's pairing code has its own five-minute life, which the contract cannot see. An approval mined after the code expired stays valid on-chain until its signature expiry but is useless; the pairing script refuses to sign or post once either clock has passed, and a fresh code needs a fresh approval. A relay that validated against stale chain state, or an enrollment completed with an earlier approval, is outside the vault's reach.
- The operator key has no other power: the vault has no generic `execute`. The owner-supplied calls the vault makes are the two rescues (`rescueERC721` and `rescueERC20`: a `safeTransferFrom` or `transfer` on the contract the owner names, never the seat, the reward token or the registrar) and `registerAgent(data)` to the pinned registrar (IMD's `Adapter8004`, see the registration section), the latter only while the seat is held and the agreement is open, only for the registrar's two registration functions `register(uint8,address,uint256,string)` and `register(uint8,address,uint256,string,(string,bytes)[])` (selectors `0xb68ca002`, `0x1fd8046a`, verified against the mainnet implementation on 2026-09-29), and only when the calldata names the ERC-721 standard, this vault's own collection and its token id. URI and metadata updates, wallet changes, approvals and transfers are refused, so calldata proposed by anyone cannot do more than register this seat. That is the whole guarantee: which selectors and which seat may be named. The registrar (`0xde152afb…`) is an external dependency behind an upgradeable proxy; a new implementation may reject these selectors, accept them with different behaviour, or accept them through a fallback, and the vault cannot tell the difference beyond requiring the call to succeed and return a non-zero agent id (an empty or zero answer is refused; that is a shape check, not upgrade protection). Under the implementation reviewed on 2026-09-29 the agent NFT stays with the registrar, control of the agent follows the seat's current owner (so nothing lands in the vault and nothing needs rescuing) and `register` is not payable. The registrar can be neither the seat collection nor the reward token and must have code, and the call is reentrancy-guarded.

## Registration (verified from public data, 2026-09-29)

IMD's `GET /agents/register-intent?tokenId=<seat>` returns `{to, data, chainId, agentURI}`: `to` is IMD's registrar `0xde152AfB7db5373F34876E1499fbD893A82dD336` (an ERC-1967 proxy whose implementation, `Adapter8004`, has verified source), `data` is `register(uint8 standard = 0, address collection, uint256 tokenId, string agentURI)` with `agentURI = https://api.imd.fun/agents/by-token/<seat>.json`, and `chainId` is 1. The registrar's source shows what happens: it requires `IERC721(collection).ownerOf(tokenId) == msg.sender`, registers the agent in the ERC-8004 registry (`0x8004a169…`) with itself as the agent NFT's owner, binds the agent to the seat (immutably, in that implementation), and from then on answers `isController(agentId, account)` as "does `account` currently own the seat"; it clears the agent wallet; `register` is not payable. The registrar is an external dependency the vault cannot verify: it is an upgradeable proxy (implementation `0xa6D23f27…`, runtime SHA-256 `503f407c…` when reviewed) whose owner can also repoint it at another ERC-8004 registry, which does not migrate existing agents; what the vault pins is an address, not an implementation, and the vault only requires a registration call to succeed and return the agent id. Before any live registration, compare the implementation and its configured registry with the reviewed ones, and check the resulting agent and binding after the receipt. Repeating `registerAgent` creates another agent for the same seat (there is no uniqueness rule); which agent IMD uses is its off-chain decision. So while the seat sits in a vault, the vault is the seat's owner and therefore the only address that can register, and the one that controls the agent; when the seat is withdrawn, control moves with it to the owner's wallet, with no token to rescue. The seat's token id is not the agent id. IMD's off-chain bind (`POST /agents/bind {tokenId, agentId, txHash}`) follows the on-chain registration and is not verified for a vault-held seat. The fork rehearsal (`test/fork/MainnetFork.t.sol`) registers through the vault against the real registrar with calldata built in the observed register-intent format and checks the control hand-over on withdrawal.

## Reward accounting

ERC-20 transfers do not call the recipient, so the vault settles by balance difference: `settle()` allocates `balance - accounted` of the pinned reward token between owner and provider (owner gets the floor of its share, provider the remainder, rounded once per settlement, full-precision arithmetic), then records the new accounted balance. Every positive balance change of the reward token is shared, whatever its source: a misdirected top-up or a donation is split like a reward, and the vault cannot tell them apart. Every `claim()` settles first, so nothing can be claimed past an incoming transfer; `withdrawNFT` does not settle (see Custody), so settlement timing only changes base-unit rounding, never the split. A claim pays what the vault actually holds when the balance fell outside transfers, and keeps the remainder allocated. Claims are per party; neither party, and no admin, can take the other's allocation. No other token is ever settled or claimed: there is one ledger for the pinned reward token, so a token reachable through two addresses cannot be split twice and no stray NFT can be moved by a claim (the third swarm review found both under the earlier any-ERC-20 rule). Anything else that lands in the vault is the owner's to take back through the rescues; the provider has no share in it. There is no collateral deposit: the only things that enter the vault are the seat NFT (through `deposit()`) and whatever tokens anyone transfers to its address.

## Isolation

Each agreement designates one NFT and has its own balance ledger (a factory pins the collection, reward token, registrar and relay). IMD pays per agent to the holder; on 2026-09-23 it paid one aggregated transfer per wallet, which is why one seat per holder address matters: with one seat designated per vault, a payout to that address needs no attribution oracle. The contract cannot enforce provenance: an ordinary `transferFrom` can still put a second seat NFT into the vault (the owner can `rescueERC721` it; a `safeTransferFrom` of another seat is refused), and anyone can send the reward token from anywhere; every positive balance change of it is split regardless of origin. Correct routing by IMD and keeping additional NFTs out of the vault are operational assumptions, not contract guarantees.

## Exit, late rewards, provider changes

Ending the agreement, taking the NFT back, and the device disconnecting are three different events. `end()` stops new pairings; `withdrawNFT()` also returns the NFT and, on IMD's side, makes the enrolled device stale (the enrolment is tied to the holder). Rewards that reached the vault before withdrawal stay in it, allocated by the same split whenever settled. Where a payout made after withdrawal lands depends on IMD's routing (the holder at payout time, or a snapshot taken earlier): whatever reaches the owner's wallet is outside the vault, and whatever still reaches the vault is split by the same terms. There is no job or period attribution and no grace rule, so the provider's enforced share ends when payouts stop reaching the vault. A provider change is a new vault: withdraw, re-deposit into the new one, pair the new device. Anything that still lands in an ended vault is split by the same terms.

## Tokens

Rewards are IMD, a plain ERC-20. Claims check exact delivery on both sides, so a recipient-tax or sender-surcharge token is refused rather than short-paying. Balance-difference accounting assumes balances only change through transfers: with a rebasing, upgradeable or dishonest token the first claimant is paid in full and the last one gets what is left, the rest staying allocated until the balance recovers (`shortfall()` shows the gap). Pin one verified asset per factory; only that asset is ever split, and other tokens are the owner's to rescue. Hostile, paused or rebasing tokens can prevent claims; they can never prevent the seat's return.

## Trust limits (do not call this trustless)

Enforced: the split of rewards that reach the vault; the owner's unilateral exit with the NFT; the operator key's confinement to approved pairings. Not enforced: service quality, payout policy (IMD decides who gets paid and how much), rewards routed anywhere but the vault, any income at all for the host, and the host's protection against an owner who withdraws before a payout. The owner still needs to be online for each pairing approval within IMD's five-minute code window. There is no owner or provider rotation: a lost owner key strands the seat in the vault, so the owner address must be a wallet the owner can always control (a multisig works: the owner never signs a pairing digest, the operator key does); a compromised provider key can end the agreement and claim the provider's allocation, nothing more.
