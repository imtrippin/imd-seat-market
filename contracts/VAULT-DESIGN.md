# SeatVault: an NFT-holding vault that enforces reward sharing (design, 2026-09-28, vault-only since 2026-09-29)

Local prototype only. It has been rehearsed against mocks on Base Sepolia (`contracts/script/testnet-walkthrough.sh`, throwaway keys) and reviewed three times; nothing has been paired with IMD, deployed on mainnet or funded with real value.

## What the developer's information enables, and what stays unverified

The IMD developer says pairing accepts a seat held by a contract that implements ERC-1271 (`isValidSignature(bytes32, bytes)` returning `0x1626ba7e`), and that rewards go to the holder, whatever it is. If both hold, an NFT held by a vault receives that seat's rewards directly, and the vault can split every reward-token transfer that reaches it by immutable terms. That is the one thing this project could not enforce before: **enforceable allocation of rewards that actually arrive**.

Still unverified, because no end-to-end contract-holder pairing has been run: that `/pair/complete` really calls `isValidSignature` with the plain EIP-712 digest (`IdentityMD Worker` v2, chain id, NFT contract) and passes the signature bytes through unchanged; the exact shape of `GET /pair/:code` (the script reads `deviceKey`, `nonce`, `relayOrigin`, `chainId`, `nftContract`, which the public docs snapshot does not list); what the ERC-8004 registration IMD asks for looks like and how IMD binds the seat to the agent (the registry itself accepts a contract caller, verified from its implementation source); that the reward distributor keeps paying the current holder when the holder is a contract, and at which moment the holder is sampled; and how promptly a device goes stale after the NFT leaves the vault. The smallest test that settles most of this is one spare seat NFT in one vault on mainnet, with a throwaway operator key: pair, observe `/seats/:tokenId/standing`, register the agent through the vault, wait for one payout, settle and claim, then withdraw the NFT and watch the device go stale. Each step is a separately approved live action.

## Chosen design: the vault alone

One contract with one job: **SeatVault** holds exactly one seat NFT for one agreement, answers ERC-1271 for owner-approved pairings, splits arriving reward tokens by immutable basis points, and gives the NFT back to the owner on demand. A factory pins the collection, reward token, identity registry and relay so a vault cannot be misconfigured by the parties.

There is no fee and no deposit. A rental escrow (a daily fee accrued per second and drawn from an owner deposit) was built and reviewed beside the vault and then removed on 2026-09-29 for simplicity; it lives in git history and could return as an optional add-on for listings that want a fee. Without it the host's only enforced protection is the split of what reaches the vault: an idle seat pays nothing, and the owner may withdraw at any moment, including right before a payout. Hosts price those risks into their percentage and their reputation; a minimum term or a cancellation charge would be a new economic rule with its own review.

## Custody

The vault is the on-chain holder: the owner `deposit()`s (a real `safeTransferFrom` into the vault; an approval alone does nothing) and only the agreed seat is accepted from the seat collection, only from the owner, only once; any other collection's token is accepted and rescuable, because an identity registry may safe-mint one here. The owner keeps the withdrawal right: `withdrawNFT(to)` whenever the vault actually holds the seat, before or after the agreement ended, whether it arrived by deposit or by a plain transfer, with no provider signature and no admin. Withdrawal makes no call to the reward token: it ends the agreement, clears the pairing approval and moves the NFT, so nothing about the reward asset (a revert, malformed return data, a gas-burning read) can delay the seat's return. Rewards already in the vault stay allocated to the same immutable split, and anyone can settle them before or after. The provider can `end()` (no new pairings) but can never move the NFT or any token but its own allocation. There is no admin key, no sweep, no upgrade.

## Pairing authority (ERC-1271), exactly

- The vault keeps a single active approval. `approvePairing(nonce, expiresAt, relayOrigin)` by the owner computes the WorkerAuthorization digest itself from the agreed device key, its own address as `wallet`, its token, IMD's nonce, the expiry and the agreed relay, and records it with its expiry and the chain id, replacing any earlier approval. It refuses another relay, an expiry in the past or more than one hour out, a vault that does not hold the NFT, or an ended agreement. Changing the device key, revoking, ending or withdrawing clears the approval.
- `isValidSignature(hash, signature)` returns the magic value only when: the NFT is held, the agreement is open, `hash` is the active approved digest, not expired, approved on this chain id, and the signature recovers to the operator key (the host's pairing key) or the owner. The operator key must differ from both the owner's and the provider's keys, or the vault refuses to be created. Anything else returns `0xffffffff` without reverting. Consequences: no arbitrary hash is ever valid, so the operator key cannot be used to sign a token approval or a transfer; a digest approved for one nonce cannot be replayed for another; a digest from another chain, relay, device, wallet or token is a different hash and is never approved; after exit or withdrawal nothing validates; a leaked operator key can at most complete a pairing the owner already approved.
- Revoking an approval, ending, or changing the device key only prevents new pairings. A device IMD already enrolled stays enrolled until IMD sees the holder change, which is what withdrawing the NFT does. Nothing in the vault disconnects a device.
- Two clocks. The vault only knows the signature expiry it approved (at most one hour); IMD's pairing code has its own five-minute life, which the contract cannot see. An approval mined after the code expired stays valid on-chain until its signature expiry but is useless; the pairing script refuses to sign or post once either clock has passed, and a fresh code needs a fresh approval. A relay that validated against stale chain state, or an enrollment completed with an earlier approval, is outside the vault's reach.
- The operator key has no other power: the vault has no generic `execute`. The only owner-supplied call the vault makes is `registerAgent(data)` to the pinned identity registry, and only while the seat is held and the agreement is open. It is an owner-only call facility, not a register-only allowlist: any calldata of at least four bytes goes through. The mainnet registry (`0x8004a169…`, an upgradeable proxy, so a pinned address is not a pinned implementation) exposes `register()`, `register(string)` and `register(string,(string,bytes)[])`, but also metadata updates and agent-NFT transfers, so IMD's proposed calldata must be decoded and checked against it before sending. Registration safe-mints a separate agent NFT to the caller and records the caller as its agent wallet; the seat's token id is not the agent id, and IMD's seat-to-agent binding is a separate step. Rescuing that agent NFT changes its owner and clears its registered wallet, so it is not an inert collectible. The registry can be neither the seat collection nor the reward token and must have code, and the call is reentrancy-guarded.

## Reward accounting

ERC-20 transfers do not call the recipient, so the vault settles by balance difference: `settle(token)` allocates `balance - accounted` between owner and provider (owner gets the floor of its share, provider the remainder, rounded once per settlement, full-precision arithmetic), then records the new accounted balance. Every positive balance change of a token is shared, whatever its source: a misdirected top-up or a donation is split like a reward, and the vault cannot tell them apart. Every `claim(token)` settles first, so nothing can be claimed past an incoming transfer; `withdrawNFT` does not settle (see Custody), so settlement timing only changes base-unit rounding, never the split. Claims are per party; neither party, and no admin, can take the other's allocation. Any ERC-20 that lands in the vault can be settled and claimed by the same rule, so for a supported token nothing gets stuck. The vault never accepts deposits: there is no collateral to confuse with a reward.

## Isolation

One NFT per vault, one vault per agreement (a factory pins the collection, reward token, identity registry and relay). IMD pays per agent to the holder; on 2026-09-23 it paid one aggregated transfer per wallet. With a single NFT in the vault every transfer into it belongs to that seat, so attribution needs no oracle. Two seats in one holder would bring the wallet-aggregation ambiguity back. One NFT per vault does not prove the source of every transfer, only that no other seat's rewards can be mixed in.

## Exit, late rewards, provider changes

Ending the agreement, taking the NFT back, and the device disconnecting are three different events. `end()` stops new pairings; `withdrawNFT()` also returns the NFT and, on IMD's side, makes the enrolled device stale (the enrolment is tied to the holder). Rewards that reached the vault before withdrawal stay in it, allocated by the same split whenever settled. Where a payout made after withdrawal lands depends on IMD's routing (the holder at payout time, or a snapshot taken earlier): whatever reaches the owner's wallet is outside the vault, and whatever still reaches the vault is split by the same terms. There is no job or period attribution and no grace rule, so the provider's enforced share ends when payouts stop reaching the vault. A provider change is a new vault: withdraw, re-deposit into the new one, pair the new device. Anything that still lands in an ended vault is split by the same terms.

## Tokens

Rewards are IMD, a plain ERC-20. Claims check exact delivery on both sides, so a recipient-tax or sender-surcharge token is refused rather than short-paying. Balance-difference accounting assumes balances only change through transfers: with a rebasing, upgradeable or dishonest token the first claimant is paid in full and the last one bears the loss (`shortfall(token)` shows the gap). Pin one verified asset per factory. Hostile, paused or rebasing tokens can prevent claims; they can never prevent the seat's return.

## Trust limits (do not call this trustless)

Enforced: the split of rewards that reach the vault; the owner's unilateral exit with the NFT; the operator key's confinement to approved pairings. Not enforced: service quality, payout policy (IMD decides who gets paid and how much), rewards routed anywhere but the vault, any income at all for the host, and the host's protection against an owner who withdraws before a payout. The owner still needs to be online for each pairing approval within IMD's five-minute code window.
